The 12 Week Year

Execution over intention

📚 What Is the 12 Week Year?

The 12 Week Year is a planning and execution system created by Brian P. Moran and Michael Lennington. Instead of setting annual goals that lose momentum by February, you compress your year into 12-week cycles.

"The number one thing that you will need to do differently is to change how you think about time. Most people think in terms of a 12-month year. The 12 Week Year redefines your year to be 12 weeks long."

Each cycle has 12 active weeks followed by a 13th week for reflection and planning. This creates urgency, focus, and predictability that annual planning simply cannot.

🎯 Why It Works

  • Urgency: 12 weeks is short enough that every week matters. There is no "I'll catch up later" mindset.
  • Focus: With a shorter horizon, you naturally narrow your goals to what truly matters.
  • Predictability: Weekly execution scoring gives you early warning signals, not year-end surprises.
  • Accountability: Weekly check-ins force honest assessment of progress.
  • Fresh starts: Every 13 weeks, you get a clean slate to adjust and improve.
"Execution is the single greatest market differentiator. Great companies and successful individuals execute better than their competition."

📌 Setting Your Vision

Before setting goals, define your long-term vision. Where do you want to be in 3-5 years? Your vision provides direction and motivation for each 12-week cycle.

A compelling vision should be:

  • Specific and vivid — paint a clear picture of your desired future
  • Personal — tied to what genuinely matters to you
  • Challenging but achievable — stretches you beyond comfort

Your 12-week goals should be stepping stones toward your vision, not disconnected tasks.

🌱 Goals: The 12 Week Targets

Each cycle should have 2-3 goals maximum. More than that dilutes focus and reduces execution quality.

Effective 12-week goals are:

  • Specific and measurable — you should know unambiguously whether you achieved them
  • Achievable in 12 weeks — ambitious but realistic within the timeframe
  • Aligned with your vision — each goal moves you closer to the bigger picture

Key Goals define what you want to achieve. Tactics define how you will achieve it.

⚙️ Tactics: The Weekly Actions

Tactics are the specific, repeatable actions you commit to executing each week to drive progress toward your goals. They are your lead measures — the inputs you control.

  • Be specific: "Write 500 words daily" not "Write more"
  • Be actionable: Each tactic should be something you can do this week
  • Be measurable: You should be able to score your execution honestly
  • Limit per goal: 3-5 tactics per goal keeps things manageable
"Knowledge alone is never enough. It's the consistent application of knowledge that produces results."

📈 Lead vs Lag Measures

Understanding the difference between lead and lag measures is critical:

  • Lag measures are the results — revenue, weight lost, product shipped. You cannot directly control them.
  • Lead measures are the activities that drive the results — sales calls made, workouts completed, code written. You can control them.

Your tactics are lead measures. Your goals are lag measures. Focus your weekly energy on executing tactics (lead), and the goals (lag) will follow.

Concept The weekly execution score measures your lead indicators. If your score is consistently above 80%, results will come.

📊 The Weekly Execution Score

The weekly score is the percentage of your tactics you executed that week. This is the single most important metric in the 12 Week Year.

  • 80%+ = On track — You are executing at a high level. Results will follow.
  • 60-79% = At risk — Execution is inconsistent. Identify and remove barriers.
  • Below 60% = Off track — Urgent action needed. Simplify your plan or address obstacles.
"The key to success in the 12 Week Year is not perfection, but consistent execution at 80% or above."

Key 80% is the target, not 100%. Perfection is not the goal — consistent, high-quality execution is.

💬 The Weekly Accountability Meeting (WAM)

The Weekly Accountability Meeting is a brief, structured check-in (ideally with a peer or group) that drives accountability and problem-solving.

Each week, answer these five questions:

  • What did you accomplish last week? — Report your results honestly
  • Any lessons, insights, or take-aways? — Reflect on what you learned
  • What is your plan for this week? — Select the tactics you want to focus on from the checklist, and add any additional comments. Your selected tactics will automatically pre-populate the next week's check-in as active tactics.
  • What's your biggest challenge? — Surface obstacles early
  • Where do you need help? — Ask for support before you fall behind

The WAM should be 15-20 minutes maximum. It's not a status meeting — it's an accountability and commitment session.

💡 Week 13: The Recovery & Planning Week

The 13th week is not a week off — it is a strategic pause for reflection and renewal.

Use Week 13 to:

  • Celebrate wins — Acknowledge what you achieved
  • Assess honestly — Where did execution break down and why?
  • Identify process gaps — What systems or habits need improving?
  • Plan the next cycle — Set new goals and tactics based on lessons learned
  • Recharge — Rest and reset your energy for the next 12 weeks

⭐ The 5 Disciplines of Execution

The 12 Week Year framework rests on five disciplines:

  • 1. Vision — Create a compelling picture of the future
  • 2. Planning — Set 12-week goals with specific tactics
  • 3. Process Control — Use weekly routines and tools to stay on track
  • 4. Measurement — Track lead and lag indicators weekly
  • 5. Time Use — Intentionally allocate time to strategic priorities

Concept Process control is what My Plan+ provides — a lightweight system for planning, tracking, and scoring your execution each week.
